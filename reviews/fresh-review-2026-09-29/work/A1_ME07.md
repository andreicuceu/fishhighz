## 1. Survey assumptions (§II A, §III)
- Square survey, area $A$, comoving quasar density $n_q$, evolution ignored across the bin (quantities taken at central redshift; test with two z-bins gives slightly *smaller* combined error, §III).
- Luminosity function $dn_q/dm(z)$ from ref. [28]. Observed band $\lambda_{\min}<\lambda<\lambda_{\max}$, $1+z=\lambda/\lambda_\alpha$, $\lambda_\alpha=1216$ Å.
- Rest-frame forest range $1041<\lambda_{\rm rest}<1185$ Å; path length $L_q\sim330\,h^{-1}$Mpc.
- Fiducial: 3900-5229 Å ($2.2<z<3.3$), $\bar z\sim2.8$, 2000 deg$^2$ (WFMOS-like), R=2000; $g<(21,22,23,24,25)$ gives $(8,20,41,77,136)$ QSO/deg$^2$; sky-dominated S/N per Å $=(11,4.5,1.8,0.7,0.3)$ (Fig. 1). The 300 deg$^2$ variant (Fig. 3) has S/N $=(32,13,5.1,2.0,0.8)$. Headline: $g=23$, S/N=1.8/Å, R>250, 2000 deg$^2$ gives 1.4% on both scales. LBG option: $g=R+0.7$; $(0.3,116,2325)$ per deg$^2$ for $g<(23,24,25)$.
- Noise is tracked over the full source count distribution (S/N as a function of $m$), not at the limiting magnitude only.
- Errors scale as $A^{-1/2}$ exactly (§III). At fixed time, wider and noisier is better; $A\propto({\rm S/N})^{-2}$ at fixed integration (Fig. 4).
- About 40 million pixels in the fiducial survey (§II B), which is why brute-force Eq. (9) is rejected.

## 2. Resolution and pixel conventions (§II A)
- rms resolution $\sigma_R$, ${\rm FWHM}=2.355\,\sigma_R=\lambda/R$. Pixel full width $l_p=\sigma_R$. Rms noise per pixel $\sigma_N$ in units of the mean transmitted flux $\bar F$.
- The paper never writes the resolution/pixel window explicitly (no $W(k_\parallel)$ formula, no sinc). The only quantitative statement: at R=125, power suppression at $k=0.07\,h/$Mpc, $\mu=0.5$ is 11%. AMBIGUOUS: whether the suppression is $e^{-k_\parallel^2\sigma_R^2}$ on power, whether pixel convolution is included, and whether $\sigma_R$ is in velocity or comoving units. $l_p$ is used as a length along the line of sight in Eq. (17).
- Aliasing noise is itself suppressed by limited resolution (§III), consistent with $P_F^{1D}$ being the resolution-smoothed 1D power.

## 3. Fisher formalism (§II B)
$F_{ij}=\langle\partial^2\mathcal L/\partial p_i\partial p_j\rangle$ (7), $2\mathcal L=\ln\det C+\delta^TC^{-1}\delta$ + const (8), $F_{ij}=\tfrac12{\rm Tr}[C^{-1}\partial_iC\,C^{-1}\partial_jC]$ (9).
- Galaxy case: Fourier modes diagonal for $\Delta k_j=2\pi/L_j$, $C_{ii}=\tfrac12[P_g(k_i;p)+P_N]$, $P_N=1/\bar n_g$. (The "$\tfrac12$" is per real or imaginary part.) The mode-count integral is not written out; "simple formulas for the Fisher matrix as an integral over k can be derived" (cites [17], Seo & Eisenstein 2003). AMBIGUOUS: no explicit $V_{\rm eff}$ or $\int d^3k$ expression is given for the forest.
- Data vector: Fourier modes of the weighted field
  $\delta_w(x)=\frac{w(x)}{\bar w}[\delta_F(x)+\delta_N(x)]$ (10), with $\delta_F=F/\bar F-1$, $F=e^{-\tau}$. Weight is zero where no sightline exists.
- $\langle\delta_k\delta_{k'}\rangle=\int dx\,dx'\,e^{ik\cdot x+ik'\cdot x'}[\xi_F(x-x')+\xi_N(x;x')][1+\delta_w(x)][1+\delta_w(x')]$ (11), $\delta_w=w/\bar w-1$, $\xi_N=\langle\delta_N\delta_N\rangle$.
- Weight correlations: $\langle\delta_w(x)\delta_w(x')\rangle=P_w^{2D}\delta_D^{(2)}(x_\perp-x'_\perp)$ (12) [reconstructed]. Transverse pixels uncorrelated; radially perfectly correlated within a spectrum (equal noise per pixel in a spectrum).
- **Observed power** (13):
  $\langle\delta_k\delta_{k'}\rangle\simeq(2\pi)^3\delta_D(k+k')\,[\,P_F^{3D}(k)+P_F^{1D}(k_\parallel)\,P_w^{2D}+P_N^{\rm eff}\,]$.
  $P_F^{1D}$ is the measured single-sightline 1D flux power; the middle term is the aliasing from discrete transverse sampling. Different $k$ modes taken uncorrelated.

## 4. Weight moments and noise normalisation (Eqs. 14-19)
Discretise transverse plane in cells of side $l\to0$; $dn_q/dm\,L_q\,l^2$ is the probability that a cell is probed by a QSO of apparent magnitude $m$.
- $\bar w=\int^{m_{\max}}dm\,\frac{dn_q}{dm}L_ql^2\,w(m)\equiv l^2L_qI_1$ (14)
- $\overline{w^2}=\int dm\,\frac{dn_q}{dm}L_ql^2\,w^2(m)\equiv l^2L_qI_2$ (15)
- $P_w^{2D}=\sigma_w^2l^2$, $\sigma_w^2=\overline{w^2}/\bar w^2-1$; as $l\to0$: $P_w^{2D}=\dfrac{I_2}{I_1^2L_q}$ (16)
- $P_N^{\rm eff}=\dfrac{I_3\,l_p}{I_1^2L_q}$ (17), $I_3\equiv\int dm\,\frac{dn_q}{dm}\,\sigma_N^2(m)\,w^2(m)$ (18)
- $I_1,I_2$ defined by (14),(15) with the $l^2L_q$ factors stripped (i.e. $I_k=\int dm\,\frac{dn_q}{dm}w^k$); the paper writes $I_1$ implicitly. Units of $dn_q/dm$ then are per unit transverse area (angular/comoving), while $L_q$ is a length: AMBIGUOUS dimensionally, since §II A calls $n_q$ a "comoving number density". Effective pixel density is $I_1L_q/l_p$ and effective pixel variance $I_3/I_1$.
- Pixel noise $\sigma_N$ enters through $\sigma_N^2(m)$ per pixel of width $l_p$; conversion to power is the white-noise rule $P=\sigma^2v$ (pixel volume $v$; area $l^2$ in 2D, applied radially through $l_p$). No explicit S/N-per-Å to $\sigma_N$ conversion is given; S/N is per Å, sky-dominated.
- **FKP weights** (19): $w(m)=\dfrac{P_S/P_N(m)}{1+P_S/P_N(m)}$, $=1$ in the low-noise limit; $w=0$ where no QSO.
- **Noise power level** $P_N(m)=\sigma_N^2(m)l_p/(I_1L_q)=\sigma_N^2(m)/n_p^{\rm eff}$, $n_p^{\rm eff}=I_1L_q/l_p$. Paper states $P_N$ is "not unambiguously defined"; this is a choice (reduces to $\sigma_N^2l_p/(n_qL_q)$ for uniform QSOs of equal magnitude).
- **Iteration**: $n_p^{\rm eff}$ and $w$ are mutually dependent, "we determine them using a few iterations". No convergence criterion stated.
- **Representative point**: single constant $P_S=$ total flux power of the central model at $k=0.07\,h{\rm Mpc}^{-1}$, $\mu=k_\parallel/k=0.5$, **including the aliasing term** ("formally signal, although in practice it acts as noise"). Justification: results "not very sensitive to the choice of $P_S$" after experimentation. The paper does not say whether $P_1D/n_{\rm eff}$ aliasing is evaluated self-consistently with the iterated $I_2/I_1^2$; implied yes because it is in the "total flux power". AMBIGUOUS: which $P_F^{1D}$ (resolution-smoothed or not) and $P_w^{2D}$ (from previous iteration) enter $P_S$.
- Aliasing/noise relation: the noise term is always subdominant, about 50% of signal-plus-aliasing power (§III), since noisy QSOs get low weight.

## 5. Mode counting and covariance (§II B end)
- Per mode, Gaussian $C=\tfrac12[P_{\rm tot}]$ with $P_{\rm tot}$ from Eq. (13); modes uncorrelated. Weight-averaged (13) is treated as the exact power. Justification: extra covariance terms are suppressed by the number of QSOs ($A$ over area per sightline) provided the second term of (13) is not much larger than the first.
- Mode cuts: $k_\perp<$ Nyquist of effective mean sightline density (conservative; information keeps growing beyond it, acknowledged as worrying); $k>0.5\,h$Mpc$^{-1}$ removed (radial only); first $2N_{q,{\rm los}}$ discrete $k_\parallel$ values (both signs) dropped for continuum fitting ($N_{q,{\rm los}}$ = spectra needed to cover the redshift range; first 5 values for fiducial). The last two cuts make no noticeable difference. $k_\parallel$ discretisation is $2\pi/L_\parallel$.
- Maximum-likelihood analysis expected to do better; FKP-like estimate deemed adequate.

## 6. P_F model, parameters, BAO treatment (§II C, §III)
- $P_F(k;p)$: fitting formula of ref. [27] (McDonald 2003). CMBfast $\Lambda$CDM: $\sigma_8=0.897$, $\Omega_m=0.281$, $\Omega_b=0.0462$, $h=0.710$, $n_s=0.980$.
- Marginalised: $\sigma_8$, $n_s$ (amplitude, slope), $\bar F$, $T_{1.4}$, $\gamma-1$, $\beta$ (large-scale anisotropy; marginalised though predicted). Fixed: other cosmology.
- Parameters of interest: radial scale $v_s(a)=H(a)s\,a$ (1) and transverse $\theta_s(a)=s\,a/D_A(a)$ (2), with $s$ the sound horizon (6). They rescale the linear spectrum from Mpc/$h$ to observed velocity/angle coordinates. Curved: $(1+z)D_A=R\sinh(r/R)$ (4), $R=c/(H_0\sqrt{\Omega_k})$ (5).
- NOT in the paper: wiggle-only differentiation (Seo & Eisenstein 2007), $d\mu/d\alpha$ handling, explicit $\Sigma_\parallel,\Sigma_\perp$ nonlinear damping, or a broadband polynomial nuisance. Full $P_F$ is differentiated; broadband is controlled by free $\sigma_8$, $n_s$, $\bar F$, $\beta$, etc. The paper says nothing on damping (linear-bias model on large scales, citing [27]) or on $\mu$ remapping. Test: with $\Omega_b=0.001$ errors are always >5%, showing the constraint comes from the baryon feature. Insensitive to dropping $\beta$ marginalisation or adding noise-amplitude marginalisation. For the Planck combination, separate slope/amplitude for CMB and Lyα so that only BAO information is used (§IV A).
- Validation: galaxy-survey limit (drop aliasing, cell noise $=1/$mean galaxies per cell, galaxy $P$) gives 1.5% ($D_A$), 1.6% ($H$) vs WFMOS 1.5%, 1.8% (with $\Omega_m=0.35$, $\sigma_8=0.9$, $b=3.3$, $h=0.65$, $n=1.0$).
- Fig. 2: bands $\Delta k=0.02\,h$Mpc$^{-1}$, $\mu$ bins $>2/3$, $1/3$-$2/3$, $<1/3$; aliasing dilutes the BAO amplitude most in transverse modes.

## 7. Explicitly stated approximations
1. Linear bias for the forest on BAO scales (§I), no evolution across the bin.
2. Gaussian modes, uncorrelated between $k$, covariance $\tfrac12[P+\ldots]$ from the weight-averaged power (Eq. 13).
3. Weight averaging done before Fisher computation; corrections $O(1/N_{\rm QSO})$.
4. Weights uniform along a sightline, transverse pixels uncorrelated; sightlines randomly (Poisson) distributed, distinct.
5. Cross-noise: $\xi_N$ diagonal in pixels, only per-pixel white noise; continuum errors handled only by dropping the lowest radial modes; fluxing errors and radial power assumed projectable out (§V); no metal/DLA/HCD contamination modelled.
6. $P_S$ fixed constant; results insensitive.
7. Only $k_\perp$ below effective Nyquist and $k<0.5$.
8. Fisher matrix (Gaussian) errors may be unreliable when errors are large (Planck-only rows, §IV A).
