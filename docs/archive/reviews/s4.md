# S4 — Direct attribution of fixed-compatibility/accuracy differences

The mixed forest–galaxy BAO damping convention is the dominant measured
contribution to the smaller revised-accuracy joint errors. The full AP derivative
prescription partly opposes that reduction. Both endpoints already use the early
lyaforecast recurrence: the difference is not a change of weighting method.
This gain predominantly reflects a changed mixed-pair reconstruction/damping
assumption, not numerical refinement alone. S4 measures that assumption's effect;
it does not validate the reconstruction physics. Scientific acceptance and
selection of further changes remain with the user.

Across the six bins, changing mixed-pair damping alone reduces joint radial
errors by **2.3760–4.1054%** and transverse errors by **0.9541–2.3831%**.
Changing the derivative prescription alone instead increases them by
**1.0198–1.1741%** and **1.1232–1.3008%**, respectively. Fourier integration
reduces radial errors by 0.6309–0.8658% and transverse errors by
0.2232–0.3112%. The signed endpoint difference is therefore a cancellation
among conventions, not an additive single-cause improvement.

The measured six-bin table and complete per-spectrum numerical extraction are
in [S4 impact table](s4-impact-table.md); the source-level inventory and derivative
equations are in [S4 profile inventory](s4-profile-inventory.md). These documents
are part of this handoff. All quoted switches are diagnostic hybrids, not new
recommended forecasting profiles.

## Calculation and separation of effects

`scripts/attribute_s4.py` directly evaluates the model on each requested Fourier
grid. It uses neither the old array-swap chain nor interpolation of saved total
powers or Jacobians. CAMB's own 1000-node linear-power interpolation remains part
of the legacy model; the supplied PK/PKSB template retains its native prepared
interpolation. The 15 individually controlled differences are magnitude measure,
negative-density policy, Fourier measure, volume, mean/covariance redshift split,
resolution convention, power growth, linear-power source, covariance wiggle
damping, mixed-pair damping, peak extraction, AP derivative prescription,
pair/field response construction, conversion constants, and stopping tolerance.
Every control starts from each endpoint separately. The intermediate 425-node
rectangular measure is also tested in both directions.

Every affected forest auxiliary P/B, magnitude input and early-weight solution
is recomputed coherently. Records contain actual weights, quadrature, signed
or floored density, pixel variance, P, B, A, P_pixel, counts, status and doubled
confirmation residuals. No weight amplitudes are renormalized. The representative
mode remains (k_transverse, k_parallel)=(2.4 deg^-1, 0.00035 s/km); a separate
representative-mode sensitivity would not attribute an endpoint convention
change and was not run. Bin 1 prepares only QSO forests and QSO positions and
contracts their three spectra; bins 2–6 retain all 15 spectra. Bin-specific AP
parameters are never combined across redshift.

The redshift switch consistently changes the mean model, mean response conversion
and damping from arithmetic to geometric bin centre. Covariance, source-query
redshift, pixel wavelength and geometry already use the same geometric centre
at both endpoints; they do not change under this switch. The controlled mean-redshift change includes its changes in f, bias, damping
and response. Separately, the captured legacy covariance interpolated f at the
geometric centre from arithmetic-centre samples, whereas this reconstruction
evaluates CAMB f exactly there. That covariance interpolation difference, and
any same-coordinate reconstruction discrepancy, remain bounded by the measured
fixed-endpoint closure residual; neither is assigned a fictitious power-growth
effect.

The damping switch changes only the forest–galaxy cross convention from the
legacy forest width to the mean squared forest and reconstructed-galaxy widths.
The configured reconstruction factor and galaxy/forest auto-width prescriptions
are unchanged. Covariance wiggle damping is a separate switch and propagates
through the auxiliary auto-P used in weights. There is no invented cross noise
or overlap prescription. Pair versus field response ownership is numerically
equivalent for these configurations because the forest pixel widths agree.

The peak switch replaces the degree-8 fit to the already observed power by the
explicit template wiggle. The operator switch is a **grouped estimator change**:
AP volume prefactor, remapped RSD, remapped fixed-width anisotropic damping,
fixed observed response, and central AP differentiation replace the projected
backward k derivative. These subterm contributions were not measured separately;
the group should not be presented as an isolated AP-prefactor or damping effect.
Global reversal of both derivative signs does not change a Fisher matrix.
The linear-power-source control and explicit wiggle extraction are distinct;
intermediate source/decomposition combinations are diagnostic constructions.

For the legacy estimator on new Fourier nodes, the degree-8 polynomial is fitted
on the original 500 k nodes at each requested mu, then both the model and fitted
polynomial are evaluated directly at the new nodes. The backward difference
retains the original Delta k; its lower evaluation point is bounded by k_min,
and the native first-node derivative remains zero. Thus the forward Fourier
switch is a specified continuous extension of a grid-dependent estimator, not
a universal quadrature correction independent of estimator definition. The
Fourier-by-operator interaction and a doubled Fourier/derivative refinement
explicitly test this limitation. The exact native-grid endpoint uses the original
fit and backward operation.

The conversion-constant switch applies c=299800→299792.458 km/s to velocity
source density, forest length, pixel width and resolution. The separate volume
switch exchanges the complete recorded endpoint volumes, including their c
normalization. This avoids double counting the volume change in the constants
row. SNR clamp/floor/sentinel, raw-cell width, source support and normalization
policies otherwise agree; no unrelated policy sensitivities were introduced.

## Interactions and numerical scope

Forward means introducing one accuracy setting into fixed compatibility. Reverse
means restoring one legacy setting in accuracy; the signs therefore usually
oppose. Neither percentage shifts nor their absolute magnitudes are additive.
For each bin, the three largest forward/reverse log-effect discrepancies identify
at most three two-setting combinations. Fourier-by-operator and peak-by-operator
are additionally predeclared. This is a bounded interaction study, not a factorial
analysis or a unique partition of the total change.

Two-setting interactions use
`log[sigma_AB sigma_0/(sigma_A sigma_B)]`, component by component, for every
selected individual spectrum and the joint contraction. Remaining higher-order
interactions are not assigned to individual physical terms. The main table reports
measured forward/reverse effects and interactions directly, rather than asserting
that their sum reproduces the endpoint difference. The largest tested interaction
is 0.00413745 in natural-log units (0.413745 log-percent), below the 1%
attribution trigger but above numerical refinement. The coordinator independently
finds that the unsummed higher-order/interaction remainder relative to the sum
of all forward log-effects reaches 0.40903 log-percent jointly and 0.640053
log-percent individually; it is not evidence for a missing physical switch.

All GL single-switch controls are repeated with 128→256 k intervals, 32→64 mu
order and 2.5e-4→1.25e-4 AP step together. The magnitude quadrature retains its
S2-qualified order 16 and explicit partition at density/SNR/policy boundaries;
the rectangular-grid comparisons are separate controls. These are finite
refinement tests, not a continuum bound. The native fixed endpoint remains a
reproducibility recipe, not a numerically converged Fourier estimator.

## Evidence and validation

The main calculation contains **364 trials and 5,116 individual/joint forecasts**,
including 58 two-switch trials and 102 combined-refinement trials. All selected
single-switch forecasts retain rank two. The maximum componentwise endpoint
closure residual is **0.000128664%**, compared with the 0.1% numerical target.
The largest joint/individual BAO refinement changes are respectively
**0.0107825% / 0.0102175%**; the maximum relative joint Fisher change is
**0.000214949**, also below 0.001. The interaction configurations were not each
independently refined, so their numerical scope is the tested endpoint and
single-switch refinements, not an independently established continuum limit.

The independent checker passes **2,548 scalar selected-cell Wick checks** and
all 5,116 saved Fisher/error inversions. Its complete endpoint joint Fisher
contractions agree within **6.35e-15 relative**; individual contractions agree
bitwise. Both directions of the isolated-volume test satisfy the analytic
V^(-1/2) scaling within **2.22e-15 relative**. The seven saved cells include the
native first node and high-k end; no zero first-node derivative was discarded.


Main outputs: `.validation/s4/direct-r1/summary.json`, per-trial JSON and NPZ.
Every NPZ retains joint and individual Fisher matrices, errors, correlations,
ranks and seven selected-cell covariance operands. Endpoints retain full T/J/mode
arrays. `.validation/s4/witness/` additionally retains full bin-1 operands for
forward/reverse mixed-pair damping and operator controls, allowing an independent
full contraction of the largest effects without redundant covariance archives.
All four witness outputs reproduce every retained main-trial array bitwise;
independent full NumPy contractions agree within 3.79e-15 relative.
See `.validation/s4/witness-check.json`.
`scripts/verify_s4.py` performs independent scalar Wick checks at every retained
cell, complete NumPy-solve endpoint contractions, direct Fisher inversions, and
both-direction volume checks against sigma proportional to V^(-1/2).

Three focused tests independently check signed native-grid peak recovery,
forest/galaxy auto limits of mixed-pair squared-width averaging, and both
resolution conventions against explicit Gaussian-times-sinc response. Ruff
check and format pass for the new diagnostic/check scripts and tests. No
production module, S1 wheel, S1–S3 evidence, reference package, survey input or
scientific default was changed.
